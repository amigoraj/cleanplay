CleanPlay Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026 · App: CleanPlay for Android (com.cleanplay.app) · Developer: CleanPlay · Available worldwide

Contact: in the app, Settings → About → Help & support. A support email address will be added here before CleanPlay is released on Google Play.

The short version

  • CleanPlay works on your phone. It has no account, no ads, no analytics and no crash-reporting service, and CleanPlay has no servers that receive information about you.
  • Your browsing history, bookmarks, downloads and settings stay on your phone.
  • To block trackers and dangerous sites across your phone, CleanPlay uses Android's VPN feature. Only the names of the sites your apps look up pass through CleanPlay, and they are checked on your phone. Your passwords, messages and the pages you open do not pass through it.
  • When you buy CleanPlay Premium, Google Play handles the payment. CleanPlay never sees your card or bank details.

1. Protection across your phone (the VPN)

When you turn Protection on, Android asks you to allow CleanPlay's VPN connection. CleanPlay uses it only to see the domain names (for example ads.example.com) that apps on your phone look up before connecting to them.

  • Each name is checked on your phone against block lists of known ad, tracker and malware sites. A blocked name is answered on your phone, so the connection never happens. Ads inside other apps are not blocked; the CleanPlay browser blocks ads on web pages.
  • A name that isn't blocked is sent on for an answer to the service you choose in Settings → DNS lookups:
    • Cloudflare (encrypted) or Quad9 (encrypted): that company's DNS service, over an encrypted connection your provider can't read. Their own privacy policies apply to the lookups they answer.
  • CleanPlay does not keep a record of the names your apps look up, and never names a site it allowed. For Activity it keeps, on your phone only: daily counts (ads, trackers and threats blocked, lookups checked, and how long protection ran) with each day's most blocked domains and apps, for 30 days; and a "Recently blocked" list of up to 200 blocked domain names with the apps that asked for them, so you can see what was blocked and allow it. For the Apps screen it counts, per app, its lookups and blocks since CleanPlay last started (in memory only). None of this leaves your phone; clear it any time in Settings → Privacy → Clear activity history.
  • The rest of your internet traffic (the pages, videos and messages themselves) does not go through CleanPlay.

Android allows one VPN at a time, so turning on another VPN app turns CleanPlay's protection off.

2. The CleanPlay browser

  • History (address, title and time of the newest 1,000 visits, and how many ads, trackers and threats were blocked on each page), bookmarks and open tabs are saved in CleanPlay's private storage on your phone, encrypted, together with the small icon each site shows in its tab (only icons the pages give the browser while you visit them; no icon service is asked). In History you can delete one visit, a site, the last hour, today, the last 7 days or everything, pause history, or have it deleted automatically after 1, 7 or 30 days. Very long addresses are saved without the part after "?".
  • History insights (CleanPlay Pro): while Pro is on, CleanPlay also notes how long each page was on screen, whether a video or recording played, and which ad and tracker servers were blocked there, to show time per site, a privacy report and your watched videos. This is worked out and kept on your phone only and is deleted with the history. Export history saves a file only where you choose.
  • Cookies and site data are kept by the browser on your phone, as in any browser, and can be cleared in Settings → Browser data. Cookies from other sites embedded in a page (third-party cookies) are turned off.
  • Private tabs keep their cookies, logins and site data separately and delete them when you close the last private tab. They leave no history.
  • Site apps are websites you put on your Home screen; their names and addresses are kept on your phone, encrypted. One with a separate login (CleanPlay Pro) keeps its own cookies, logins and site data apart from the browser's, until you sign it out or remove it in Settings → Browser → Site apps. Pages opened in a site app go into your history like any other page.
  • Searches you type in the address bar go to the search engine chosen in Settings → Browser (DuckDuckGo unless you pick Google, Bing, Brave or Startpage), whose privacy policy applies. A site shortcut or "Search in" chip (for example "shopee table fan") sends the words to that site instead. While you type, suggestions come from your own tabs, bookmarks, history, My reading and study cases, matched on your phone; nothing you type is sent until you choose a search. On search results pages, CleanPlay checks the result addresses against its lists and your saved pages on your phone to add warnings, and opens results without the engine's tracking redirect; nothing about this is sent anywhere.
  • Site permissions: a website can use your camera, microphone or location, or play protected (copy-protected) videos, only after you allow it. What you choose to remember for a site is saved on your phone and can be removed in Settings → Browser → Site permissions; private tabs ask every time and remember nothing.
  • Clean this page: what you choose to hide "always" on a site is saved on your phone as that site's name and the part of the page to hide. The shield on that site shows it again.
  • Reader view reformats the page you are on, on your phone. Its look (page colour, typeface, spacing, width, text size) is saved on your phone. Study tools (CleanPlay Pro; Free in one study case): articles you save to My reading, with your highlights, notes and study cases (topics that group articles from several websites), are kept on your phone only, encrypted, never from private tabs; you can delete them in My reading or the Privacy Centre. Listen uses your phone's own voice and lights up the sentence being read.
  • Continue where you stopped (CleanPlay Pro): for videos and recordings longer than 3 minutes, the page's address and where you stopped are saved on your phone (up to 300), never for private tabs. Settings → Privacy → Clear activity history removes them.
  • Up next (CleanPlay Pro): pages you add to the queue are saved on your phone (address and title, up to 100) until they play or you remove them. Nothing is downloaded; the site plays it as usual.
  • Websites you visit receive your requests, as in any browser. CleanPlay sends them a shortened description of your browser that leaves out your phone's model.
  • Downloads are carried out by Android's own download manager and saved in your phone's Download folder. They are yours to keep and delete.
  • My Media plays the videos and audio you downloaded, on your phone, and remembers where you stopped in each (on your phone only).
  • Download video looks for the file the page itself plays and asks that site for its size before you download; nothing goes anywhere else.
  • My block list (CleanPlay Pro): the sites you add are saved on your phone.
  • Private vault (CleanPlay Pro): files you move into it are kept only inside CleanPlay on your phone, encrypted with a key that can't leave the phone, and opened only with your screen lock. They aren't backed up anywhere: uninstalling CleanPlay or clearing its storage deletes them for good.
  • Ad Balance (Settings → What to block): Strict, Balanced or Support decides whether ads may show in the CleanPlay browser; trackers, threats and pop-ups stay blocked in every position. Sites you support (from the shield) are saved on your phone, encrypted, and never from private tabs. The shield can list which ad companies a page's ads came from; this is worked out from the page on your phone and sent nowhere. No one pays CleanPlay to have ads let through.
  • Kindness Rewards (Settings): badges worked out on your phone from counts it keeps: sites you support, days with Ad Balance, reports you chose to send, days CleanPlay protected you, and how often you shared CleanPlay (not with whom). No points or money; nothing is sent.
  • Privacy lock (CleanPlay Pro) uses your phone's own screen lock (fingerprint, face or PIN); Android checks it, and CleanPlay never sees or stores it.
  • Listen to articles (CleanPlay Pro) uses your phone's text-to-speech voice. Weekly privacy report (CleanPlay Pro) is worked out on your phone from the Activity counts.
  • Safe Browsing: Android's browser engine checks the pages you open against Google's lists of malware and phishing sites. This is done by Google Play services using shortened codes (partial hashes) of addresses, not the addresses themselves. The browser engine's own usage statistics are switched off in CleanPlay.
  • When you choose "Open in Chrome", the page's address is handed to Chrome, whose own policies then apply.

3. Media playback

When a page plays audio or video, CleanPlay shows the title, the site or channel name and a thumbnail picture on your lock screen, in the notification shade and on connected Bluetooth devices. When enabled, the thumbnail is downloaded over HTTPS from the address the page publishes, checked against the browser's current filtering rules, and kept in memory only. In Settings → Privacy, turn off "Show video thumbnails on the lock screen" to stop new thumbnail requests and clear cached artwork from the lock screen and media notification. Playback controls stay available. A request already in progress may have reached the server before it is cancelled; its late result is discarded. The switch is on by default. In a private tab, these show only "Playing in a private tab".

6. Children

CleanPlay is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. It collects no personal information from anyone.

7. Keeping and deleting your data

Everything CleanPlay stores is on your phone, in storage other apps can't read, and is not backed up to the cloud by CleanPlay. You can delete it in the app (history, cookies and site data, allowed sites, downloads), or all at once by uninstalling CleanPlay or clearing its storage in Android's settings. CleanPlay holds nothing about you elsewhere, so there is nothing else to delete.

8. Your rights, wherever you live

Privacy laws in many places, for example the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), give you rights over personal data held about you: to see it, correct it, delete it, or object to how it is used. CleanPlay holds no personal data about you outside your own phone, and it does not sell or share personal data. The data on your phone is under your control, as described in section 7. If you have a question or a request, use Help & support in the app; we will answer within 30 days.

CleanPlay works the same in every country. It does not move your data between countries, because it doesn't receive any. The lists it downloads (section 4) and the DNS services you choose (section 1) are run by their own providers, which may be located in other countries.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this address with a new "Last updated" date. Important changes will also be shown in the app.

10. Contact

Questions or requests: in the app, open Settings → About → Help & support. A support email address will be added here before CleanPlay is released on Google Play. Messages you send reach us through your own email app, and we use them only to answer you.
